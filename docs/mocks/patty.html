Design notes

  • Shape: a short, plump coccobacillus, a bit smaller than Coco, since both are tiny Gram-negative rods from the Pasteurellaceae family.
  • No flagella: she’s non-motile, so she never gets any.
  • Bunny ears: tiny ones on top (an accessory, like Penny’s glasses). They’re a nod to “snuffles,” the sneezy, snotty nose infection she’s best known for in pet rabbits. Real cells have no ears!
  • Color: a soft coral with a terracotta outline. It sits between Sara’s bright red, Vi’s orange and Diffany’s chestnut without matching any of them; worth checking side by side in the picker.

Fun-fact candidates

  • She’s named for Louis Pasteur, who studied her as the cause of fowl cholera and, around 1880, made one of the first vaccines in a lab from weakened cultures of her.
  • multocida means “kills many” in Latin, since she infects so many different animals: birds, cattle, pigs, rabbits, cats and dogs.
  • She lives harmlessly in the mouths of many cats and dogs, so infected bites are often hers, and they can get red and swollen within a day.
  • Unlike most Gram-negative bacteria, she’s usually killed by plain penicillin.
  • Her colonies have a musty, “mousy” smell.
